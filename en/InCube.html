In.cube program

From American Biodesign to the real Brazil.

How to train health teams to build solutions that survive Brazilian regulation, markets and fundraising, not just Demo Day. Finalist, Don Norman Design Award 2024, Education category, and honorable mention at the International Open Innovation Congress.

§ Context

A hospital that trains startups

In.cube started as a 4-month program designed by Wylinka, with a generic acceleration method and no learning modules specific to health projects. Today it runs for 8 months with three learning modules and partnerships built into the curriculum: Brazil's health regulator (Anvisa), the National Institute of Industrial Property (INPI) and the São Paulo Research Foundation (FAPESP).

§ My role

Observer first, then redesign lead

I followed cohorts 1 and 2 as an observer, documenting learnings before taking over. From the 3rd cohort on, I led the program's redesign and management.

§ Discovery

What two cohorts of observation revealed

The solution phase stopped at generic concepts, with no prototyping or user testing. Interviews happened, but with generic guidance and no stakeholder mapping. And nothing prepared teams for regulation, the market or fundraising in Brazil.

§ Diagnosis

Biodesign wasn't missing. Brazil was.

§ Direction: the three learning modules

What each module gained that is specific to Brazilian healthcare

01

Discovering the problem

From generic interviews to stakeholder mapping: robust research tools tailored to each type of health project.

02

Designing the solution

Prototyping and Test Day sessions with real users were added, neither existed before, along with mentoring on the regulatory pathway and clinical research.

03

Modeling the business

A partnership with INPI on intellectual property and mentoring from FAPESP for applications to PIPE 1 (Innovative Research in Small Businesses). Neither existed before.

§ Before
  • —Solutions stopped at concept: no prototyping, no testing
  • —Interviews with generic guidance, no stakeholder mapping
  • —No connection to the regulator, patent office, funding agency or clinical research
  • —A single pitch panel for software, hardware and biotech
§ After
  • →Prototyping and Test Days with real users
  • →Robust research tools and stakeholder mapping
  • →Regulator, clinical research, patent office and FAPESP mentoring (PIPE 1)
  • →Panels by technology type and angel investors at Pitch Day
§ Trade-off

Free for participants, paid for those who only want the content

Keeping the program free for participants required outside funding: a partnership with FAPESP and sponsorship from Unimed Nacional. The online track, created to sustain engagement between sessions, became a paid product for people who only want the content, generating revenue for the program.

Pessoas que Transformam

A decision tree to guide the city's transformation projects.

Design of the City of São Paulo's public service improvement program: qualitative diagnosis with civil servants, an eight-stage modular journey and a decision tree to route public transformation projects.

§ My role

Diagnosis, modular journey and product menu.

I ran the qualitative diagnosis with civil servants to understand where the city's transformation projects got stuck, and designed an eight-stage modular journey, from initial idea to project in execution, with a decision tree that routes each civil servant to the right product for their project's stage.

More than 20 products and tools were delivered as part of this modular menu.

§ Process

Framework from outside, calibration from inside

There was no previous program to redesign. I started from workshops with the city department and from service design frameworks for public policy, drawing on Lucy Kimbell and Lara Penin, and we adapted those models together with the department's team until they described what the team can actually implement, not what the literature recommends.

§ Decisions

Two choices shaped the program

  • Modular structureCity departments had very different maturity levels. A single path would leave half the teams behind. Modules let each team enter at the right point instead of everyone starting from scratch.
  • Management deliverables, not just design onesJourneys and blueprints don't survive on their own inside the public machine. I included an action plan, a schedule with a work breakdown structure and a staffing plan, so implementation would have an owner, a deadline and a team.

In.spire program

Patients and caregivers designing change alongside the people who care for them.

A service transformation training journey for hospital staff, structured around the Double Diamond, with patients and caregivers taking part directly in mapping challenges.

§ My role

Workshop facilitation and group mentoring.

I designed and facilitated the training journey following the Double Diamond, discover, define, develop and deliver, with sessions that put patients and caregivers side by side with hospital staff to map real care challenges.

The program has run three cohorts, with group mentoring supporting each team through to a solution prototype.

NPS 88
Participant satisfaction across 3 cohorts
§ Decision

Patients at the design table, next to the people who care for them.

The conventional path would be to hear patients during discovery and hand the material to professionals to work with. I brought patients and caregivers into the workshops, into the design moments, sitting with the teams. The cost is known: sessions that are harder to facilitate and a power imbalance in the room. The gain was seeing problems the clinical view can't reach.

§ What only surfaced this way

The common room

The common room layout caused interpersonal conflict between different patient profiles, including people with substance use disorders. The arrangement was changed to reduce friction and make room for different profiles in the same space.

§ Outcome

The measure is what the team manages to put in place in its own service

On average, 3 out of every 5 projects per cohort left the journey with at least a minimal implementation inside the hospital. The program's success criterion isn't the project presented at the end, it's what the team manages to put in place in its own service.
